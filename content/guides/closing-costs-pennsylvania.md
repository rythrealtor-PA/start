## The realty transfer tax

Pennsylvania charges a **1% state realty transfer tax** on the sale price, and most municipalities and school districts add a local transfer tax, often **1% combined**, though some places charge more. By custom, buyer and seller split the total, but who pays what is negotiable.

**Example:** on a $300,000 home with a 2% total transfer tax, the tax is $6,000, usually $3,000 from the buyer and $3,000 from the seller.

## What buyers usually pay

- **Your share of the transfer tax**
- **Lender fees:** origination, underwriting and credit report
- **Appraisal fee**
- **Title insurance and settlement fees.** Title insurance premiums in Pennsylvania follow rates filed with the state, and a lender's policy is required with a mortgage.
- **Recording fees** for the deed and mortgage
- **Prepaid items:** homeowners insurance, property taxes and interest, plus an escrow account
- **POA or HOA fees,** such as a capital contribution or transfer fee, where the community charges one
- **Inspections,** usually paid before settlement

## What sellers usually pay

- **The agent's commission,** which is negotiable
- **Your share of the transfer tax**
- **Mortgage payoff** and the fee to release the lien
- **Prorated property taxes**
- **Municipal certifications** (such as a use and occupancy inspection) and the POA or HOA resale certificate
- **Repairs or credits** agreed to after the buyer's inspection

## Property tax prorations

Pennsylvania property taxes come in separate bills: county and municipal taxes are usually billed in the spring, and school taxes in the summer or fall. At settlement, the taxes are prorated so buyer and seller each pay for the part of the year they own the home.

## Ways to lower your closing costs

- **Ask for a seller credit** toward your closing costs as part of your offer.
- **Use PHFA assistance** if you qualify. See the [First-time home buyer guide](first-time-home-buyer-pennsylvania.html).
- **Compare lenders.** Fees and rates vary, and the Loan Estimate makes them easy to compare.
- **Negotiate the transfer tax split** in a buyer's or seller's market.

## Questions

### Who pays the transfer tax in Pennsylvania?

By custom, buyer and seller split it equally, but it is negotiable and is set in the agreement of sale.

### Is the transfer tax 2% everywhere in Pennsylvania?

No. The state portion is 1% everywhere, but the local portion varies by municipality and school district, and some places charge more than 1%. Ryth confirms the exact rate for each home.

### Are closing costs negotiable?

Many are. The transfer tax split, seller credits and some lender and title fees can be negotiated, and comparing lenders helps.
