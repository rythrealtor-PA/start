## Step 1: Check the rules first

This is the step that sinks the most deals. Before you make an offer, confirm three things.

- The township's ordinance. Most Pocono townships require a permit or license, an inspection, occupancy limits and a local contact person, and some allow rentals only in certain zones.
- The community's rules. Many POAs restrict or prohibit short-term rentals, or charge fees for them, even where the township allows them.
- What the license allows. Plan your numbers on the bedrooms and guests the license permits, not on the occupancy in the current listing.

Ryth checks both the township and the community rules before you commit.

## Step 2: Taxes you will collect and pay

- Pennsylvania's 6% hotel occupancy tax, on stays of fewer than 30 consecutive days.
- Monroe County's 3% hotel room excise tax, which the county applies to homes rented for overnight stays.
- Income tax on your rental income. A tax professional can also explain depreciation and, if you are selling another investment property, a 1031 exchange.

## Step 3: Location

Guests want to be close to everything the Poconos have, without a long drive from New Jersey or New York. That is why the homes near the big attractions book the most nights: Camelback Resort and The Crossings Premium Outlets in Tannersville, Kalahari Resorts in Pocono Manor, Pocono Raceway in Long Pond, Mount Airy Casino Resort near Mount Pocono, and the shopping areas around them. Easy access from I-80 and I-380 matters too.

Guests who come for nature look for homes near Big Pocono State Park, Tobyhanna State Park, Marshalls Falls Park in Smithfield Township, the Dunnfield Creek trailhead just across the Delaware Water Gap in New Jersey, and the many lakes where they can fish, swim, go boating and kayak. In winter, homes within about 20 minutes of a ski area get more bookings.

## Step 4: Features guests look for

{{photos}}

Guests compare photos, bedrooms and amenities before they book, and the homes with something special earn more.

- A hot tub and a fireplace
- A game room or a space for groups
- Lake access, or community amenities such as pools and beaches
- Fast Wi-Fi and a place to work
- Enough bedrooms, beds and parking for groups, within what the license allows

## Step 5: Run the real numbers

AirDNA data for the 12 months to August 2026 put the average Pocono short-term rental at about $55,200 in revenue, with 43% occupancy and an average nightly rate of about $397. By area, averages ranged from about $45,600 around Jim Thorpe to about $62,300 around East Stroudsburg. Revenue is not profit.

Estimate the income from comparable rentals nearby, season by season, and then subtract every cost: mortgage, property taxes, insurance (including coverage for short-term rentals), POA dues, utilities, internet, cleaning, supplies, maintenance, platform fees, management if you use it, license fees and a reserve for repairs. A home that looks profitable at peak-season rates may not be once the slow months, such as March and April, are counted.

## Step 6: Financing

Financing a vacation rental is different from financing a primary home. Second-home and investment-property loans usually require a larger down payment, and some lenders offer loans based on the property's expected rental income. Talk to a lender early so the numbers you run are real.

## Questions

### Which Monroe County townships allow short-term rentals?

In Ryth's assessment as of October 2026, the easiest are Tunkhannock, Tobyhanna, Barrett and Middle Smithfield townships. Polk, Eldred, Jackson, Stroud and Price townships allow them with limits or have unsettled rules. Ryth rates the other eleven municipalities, including the boroughs of Stroudsburg and East Stroudsburg, as very difficult. Rules change, so always confirm with the township.

### Can I put any house in the Poconos on Airbnb?

No. Many townships require a permit and an inspection, allow rentals only in certain zones, or ban them in residential areas, and many communities restrict or ban them too. Always confirm both before you buy.

### How much does a Pocono vacation rental make?

According to AirDNA, the average Pocono short-term rental brought in about $55,200 in the 12 months to August 2026, and most areas averaged between about $45,000 and $65,000. Homes with standout features can earn much more, and costs come out of that revenue.

### What taxes does a Pocono short-term rental collect?

In Monroe County, stays of fewer than 30 days are generally subject to Pennsylvania's 6% hotel occupancy tax and the county's 3% hotel room excise tax. Some booking platforms collect certain taxes for you; confirm what yours covers.

### Can I get a mortgage on a vacation rental?

Yes. Second-home and investment-property loans are common. They usually need a larger down payment than a primary home, and some lenders qualify you on the property's expected rental income.
