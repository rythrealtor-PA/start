Investing in a Pocono Vacation Rental: What to Know Before You Buy

By Ryth Vara, REALTOR® · Real of Pennsylvania · Updated October 8, 2026

In short: Before buying a vacation rental in the Poconos, confirm that both the township and the community (POA) allow short-term rentals, what permits, inspections and occupancy limits apply, and which taxes you must collect, because the rules differ from one township and community to the next. Then judge the property on its location near year-round attractions, its rental-ready features, realistic seasonal income and the full cost of owning it.

Step 1: Check the rules first

This is the step that sinks the most deals. Before you make an offer, confirm:

  • The township's short-term rental ordinance. Many Pocono townships require a permit or registration, a safety inspection, occupancy limits and a local contact person.
  • The community's rules. Many POAs restrict or prohibit short-term rentals, or charge fees for them, even where the township allows them.
  • Parking, trash and noise rules that come with the permit.

Ryth checks both the township and the community rules before you commit.

Step 2: Taxes you will collect and pay

  • Pennsylvania hotel occupancy tax. Stays of fewer than 30 consecutive days are generally subject to the state's 6% hotel occupancy tax.
  • Local room taxes, where the county or municipality charges one.
  • Income tax on your rental income. A tax professional can also explain depreciation and, if you are selling another investment property, a 1031 exchange.

Step 3: Location

Guests come to the Poconos all year: skiing and indoor water parks in winter, lakes and hiking in summer, and fall foliage. Homes near the big draws, such as Camelback in Tannersville, the lake communities of Pocono Pines, Pocono Raceway and the ski areas, tend to book more nights. Easy access from I-80 and I-380 matters to guests driving from New York, New Jersey and Philadelphia.

Step 4: Features guests look for

  • A hot tub and a fireplace
  • A game room or a space for groups
  • Lake access, or community amenities such as pools and beaches
  • Fast Wi-Fi and a workspace
  • Enough bedrooms, beds and parking for groups

Step 5: Run the real numbers

Estimate the income from comparable rentals nearby, season by season, and then subtract every cost: mortgage, property taxes, insurance (including coverage for short-term rentals), POA dues, utilities, internet, cleaning, supplies, maintenance, platform fees, management if you use it, and a reserve for repairs. A home that looks profitable at peak-season rates may not be once the slow months are counted.

Step 6: Financing

Financing a vacation rental is different from financing a primary home. Second-home and investment-property loans usually require a larger down payment, and some lenders offer loans based on the property's expected rental income. Talk to a lender early so the numbers you run are real.

What clients say

Questions

Can I put any house in the Poconos on Airbnb?

No. Many townships require a permit and inspection for short-term rentals, and many communities restrict or ban them. Always confirm both before you buy.

What taxes does a Pocono short-term rental collect?

Stays of fewer than 30 days are generally subject to Pennsylvania's 6% hotel occupancy tax, plus any local room tax. Some booking platforms collect certain taxes for you; confirm what yours covers.

Can I get a mortgage on a vacation rental?

Yes. Second-home and investment-property loans are common. They usually need a larger down payment than a primary home, and some lenders qualify you on the property's expected rental income.
