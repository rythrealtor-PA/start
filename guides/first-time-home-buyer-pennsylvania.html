First-Time Home Buyer Guide for Pennsylvania (2026)

By Ryth Vara, REALTOR® · Real of Pennsylvania · Updated October 8, 2026

In short: To buy your first home in Pennsylvania, you will usually need a mortgage pre-approval, a written buyer agreement with your agent before touring homes, a down payment (as little as 0 to 3.5% with some loan programs) and money for closing costs. From accepted offer to settlement typically takes 30 to 60 days. Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency (PHFA) programs can help with the down payment and closing costs if you qualify.

Step by step

  1. Know your budget. Look at your savings, monthly debts and credit. A lender can tell you what you qualify for, but decide what monthly payment you are comfortable with.
  2. Get pre-approved. A pre-approval letter shows sellers you can buy, and it is expected with most offers.
  3. Sign a buyer agreement. Since August 2024, buyers sign a written agreement with their agent before touring homes. It states what your agent does for you and how, and how much, they are paid. That compensation is negotiable.
  4. Find the home. Ryth sets up searches, tours homes with you and tells you what he sees in each one: condition, price and neighborhood.
  5. Make an offer. The offer includes the price, a good-faith deposit, the settlement date and contingencies that protect you, such as inspection, financing and appraisal.
  6. Inspect. A home inspection, plus radon testing, a wood-destroying insect inspection, and well and septic inspections where the home has them.
  7. Appraisal and loan approval. The lender orders an appraisal and finalizes your loan.
  8. Settlement. In Pennsylvania, settlement is usually handled by a title company. You sign, pay your closing costs and get the keys.

Loan options with low down payments

  • FHA loans: as little as 3.5% down.
  • Conventional loans: as little as 3% down for many first-time buyers.
  • VA loans: 0% down for eligible veterans and service members.
  • USDA loans: 0% down in eligible rural areas. Parts of Monroe County may qualify, depending on the address.

Help from PHFA

The Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency offers first-time buyer programs through approved lenders, including the Keystone Home Loan and the Keystone Advantage Assistance Loan, which helps with the down payment and closing costs. Income and purchase-price limits apply, and the amounts change, so check the current terms with a PHFA-approved lender. Ryth can connect you with one.

Costs to plan for

  • The down payment
  • Closing costs, often around 2 to 5% of the price, including your share of the transfer tax. See Closing costs in Pennsylvania.
  • Prepaid property taxes and homeowners insurance
  • Inspections, usually paid before settlement
  • Moving costs and a cushion for the first repairs

Pennsylvania specifics to know

  • The Consumer Notice. At your first real conversation, your agent gives you a state notice explaining the kinds of relationships you can have with an agent.
  • The Seller's Property Disclosure Statement. For most homes, the seller must tell you in writing about known problems.
  • Three property tax bills. Pennsylvania property taxes go to the county, the township or borough, and the school district.
  • POA and HOA communities. In the Poconos especially, many homes belong to a community with dues and rules, and the seller provides a resale certificate.

What clients say

Questions

How much money do I need to buy my first home in Pennsylvania?

It depends on the price and the loan. With some loans the down payment can be 0 to 3.5%, and closing costs are often 2 to 5% of the price. PHFA programs can help with both if you qualify.

Do I have to pay my real estate agent as a buyer?

You will sign a written buyer agreement before touring that states exactly how, and how much, your agent is paid. Compensation is negotiable, and in many sales the seller pays part or all of it. Ryth walks you through the agreement line by line.

Can I get a mortgage with an ITIN instead of a Social Security number?

Some lenders offer mortgages to borrowers with an ITIN, usually with different terms than standard loans. Ryth can connect you with lenders who offer them.

How long does it take to buy a house in Pennsylvania?

Finding the home varies. Once your offer is accepted, settlement typically takes 30 to 60 days.
