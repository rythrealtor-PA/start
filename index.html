How do I buy a house in the Poconos?

Get pre-approved, then choose the township as well as the town: property taxes, the school district and rental rules all change from one township to the next. Most Pocono homes have a private well and septic system and many are in communities with HOA fees, so Ryth gets those inspected and the community’s rules reviewed before you commit. The steps, prices by town and second-home tips are in Buying a home in the Poconos.

Can I buy a house in the Poconos to use as an Airbnb?

Yes, in the right place. Each township decides whether short-term rentals are allowed, and many communities add their own rules, so a home that looks perfect can still be off-limits. See which townships Ryth rates green, yellow or red, what homes cost and the best towns in Buying an Airbnb in the Poconos.

Is the Poconos a good investment?

It can be, if the numbers work for the specific home. AirDNA put the average Pocono short-term rental at about $55,200 in revenue in the 12 months to August 2026, but rules, taxes and upkeep vary a lot by township, and revenue is not profit. Investing in Poconos real estate covers long-term rentals, flips and land, and Buying an Airbnb in the Poconos covers short-term rentals.

Which town in the Poconos is best to live in?

It depends on your day-to-day. Stroudsburg and East Stroudsburg put shops, restaurants and the I-80 commute close by, with public water and sewer in the boroughs; Tannersville sits next to Camelback and the outlets; Pocono Pines is lake living around Lake Naomi. The Monroe County guide compares the towns Ryth serves.

How much do I need down for a second home in Pennsylvania?

About 10% on a conventional second-home loan, under Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac rules, if you use the home yourself for part of the year; a home bought as a full-time rental needs about 15% or more. FHA, VA and USDA loans are only for a main home, and your lender confirms the exact terms. More in Buying a second home in the Poconos.

How do I sell my house in the Poconos?

Price it from recent sales in your township, fill out Pennsylvania’s seller disclosure form, and order your community’s resale certificate and any township resale inspection as soon as you list. Ryth markets every listing with professional photos, targeted ads and exposure on multiple MLS systems, in English and Spanish. The full steps are in How to sell your house in the Poconos; for Bethlehem, Easton or Allentown, see selling in the Lehigh Valley.
